Introduction
Background & Context
Chapter 1

Mandate of Student Leadership

Student governments serve as institutional bridges that nurture youth involvement, character development, and citizenship. Under Department of Education guidelines, the Supreme Secondary Learner Government (SSLG) operates as the official student leadership body in secondary schools across the Philippines.

Local Imperative at Candabong NHS

At Candabong National High School (Anda, Bohol), student leaders initiate campus projects, clean-up drives, and values programs. However, no systematic empirical study had evaluated whether students perceive these services as effective or if they meaningfully impact student growth.

Core Motivation: Transitioning student leadership from passive activity coordination into a measurable driver of positive student development and ethical maturity.
💡 Panel Point: Emphasize that evaluating SSLG services fills a critical need for evidence-based student government operations at the local school level.
Introduction
Statement of the Problem & Research Gap
Chapter 1

The Operational Gap: Tokenism vs. Transformation

In many secondary institutions, student governments risk falling into tokenization—where student leaders carry ceremonial titles without genuine administrative authority or measurable developmental output for their constituents.

Research Gap 1: Empirical Evaluation

Prior literature primarily evaluates student government from an administrative or policy perspective, with limited empirical focus on direct constituent satisfaction metrics in rural high schools.

Research Gap 2: Developmental Link

Few studies quantify how specific SSLG service dimensions (e.g., Values Formation, Safe Environment) directly correlate with constituent Behavioral Maturity and Civic Engagement.

💡 Key takeaway: Frame the research gap around moving away from token student governance toward verifiable student development outcomes.

Theoretical Underpinnings
Theoretical Framework
Chapter 1

1. Hexagon Theory of Student Leadership (Amirianzadeh, 2012)

Posits that student leadership growth is built upon six interdependent dimensions: ethical principles, strategic vision, emotional intelligence, communication, decision-making, and community engagement. This provides the theoretical backbone for measuring behavioral maturity and civic action.

2. Leadership Trait Theory (Allport, 1940)

States that effective leadership requires key personal characteristics—accountability, empathy, initiative, and integrity—that inspire trust and satisfaction among followers, establishing why quality SSLG service delivery generates constituent satisfaction.

💡 Explanation: Hexagon Theory explains the *developmental outcomes*, while Trait Theory explains why *leader actions* drive satisfaction.
Legal Mandates
Legal Framework & DepEd Policies
Chapter 1

DepEd Order No. 47, s. 2014

Mandates the Constitution and By-Laws of Supreme Student Governments in secondary schools, defining their duties in student advocacy, campus safety, and values integration.

DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012

Establishes the Child Protection Policy, charging student leadership bodies with co-creating a safe, non-violent, and inclusive school ecosystem.

Executive Order No. 200 (1969)

Secures youth rights to organize autonomous learner associations with administrative and civic functions within academic institutions.

💡 Context: Demonstrates that SSLG operations are legally grounded in national educational directives.

Normality Testing & Statistical Tools
Chapter 3

Statistical Tools Applied

  • Frequency & Percentage: Demographic profile analysis.
  • Weighted Mean & Std. Deviation: Central tendency and variability of satisfaction and development indicators.
  • Spearman’s Rank Correlation ($r_s$): Measuring ordinal relationship strength.

Normality Test Results (Shapiro-Wilk)

Shapiro-Wilk normality testing yielded $p < 0.05$ across key sub-parameters, indicating that dataset distribution significantly departed from normal curve distribution.

Methodological Justification: The violation of the normality assumption dictated the use of non-parametric statistics (Spearman’s $r_s$) over parametric alternatives (Pearson’s $r$).
💡 Critical Defense Detail: Anticipate panel questions on statistic choice—non-normality requires Spearman’s rank coefficient.

Results & Discussion
Synthesis & Theoretical Alignment
Chapter 4

Alignment with Amirianzadeh (2012)

The strong correlation ($r_s = 0.701$) validates Hexagon Theory: structured student governance directly facilitates holistic student growth across ethical and civic dimensions.

Practical Implications

Quality SSLG service delivery is not merely administrative maintenance—it serves as an informal educational driver that strengthens student discipline and civic responsibility.

Critical Insight: When student governments actively address campus environment and values, student apathy decreases and constituent participation rises proportionally.
💡 Panel Point: Connect empirical findings back to the theoretical framework established in Chapter 1.
Critical Risk Analysis
Risk Assessment: Impacts of Service Gaps
Evaluation

1. Student Cynicism & Disengagement

If SSLG service delivery declines or becomes purely ceremonial, student trust erodes, leading to lower participation in school-wide civic activities.

2. Erosion of Peer Modeling

Inconsistent character and values campaigns lead to a decline in peer-driven discipline, increasing disciplinary interventions by teachers.

3. Tokenization Risk

Failing to continuously support student governance degrades SSLG into a decorative body, squandering its potential as a leadership development incubator.

4. Administrative Overload

Without active student leadership co-managing campus climate, faculty and administrators absorb excess workload for event management and dispute resolution.

💡 Risk Perspective: Shows the panel that the researchers understand the consequences of neglecting student government quality.
Conclusions
Summary of Findings & Conclusions
Chapter 5

Summary of Key Findings

  • Profile: Sample ($n=272$) is balanced across age, sex, and grade levels.
  • Satisfaction: Students report High Satisfaction with SSLG Services ($\bar{X} = 3.10$).
  • Development: Students display High Student Development ($\bar{X} = 3.10$).
  • Correlation: Strong positive relationship ($r_s = 0.701, p < 0.05$). $H_0$ is rejected.
Conclusion: Students’ satisfaction with SSLG services is a significant determinant of student development. Enhancing character modeling and environmental safety initiatives directly accelerates constituent behavioral maturity and civic engagement.
